Your broker's CSV in. Schedule 3 out.

taxjson computes capital gains the way the CRA and IRS actually define them: one average-cost pool per security across all of your accounts, the superficial-loss rule checked against your RRSP and TFSA too, written-option premiums recognised in the year written as s.49 says, renames as dated events, splits, spin-offs and mergers handled with elections you choose. Nothing leaves your computer.

bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://taxjson.com/install.sh)"

One line on Linux or macOS; it installs taxjson and its short name tjs. Then tjs init --country canada (or usa) in an empty folder, drop your exports into inputs/, and tjs run.

How it works

Three steps, all on disk

Export

Download the activity CSV from each broker — IBKR Flex, Questrade, RBC Direct Investing, Webull, Kraken, Coinbase — and drop it into inputs/<account>/, or let the optional fetch plugin download Questrade and IBKR for you. Registered accounts are declared as sheltered in taxjson.toml.

Run

tjs run parses every file, converts at the Bank of Canada rate for each settlement date (Valet API), pools identical securities, applies renames, splits and corporate actions on their dates, then runs the superficial-loss pass across taxable and registered accounts.

Read

Per-account summaries, Schedule 3 or Form 8949 rows, the wash radar ("safe to sell at a loss?"), harvest candidates, T1135, a tax and minimum-tax estimate land in reports/ and behind the commands. Every number traces back to the rows that produced it.

A project is two files you edit. taxjson.toml holds the tax year, country, province, accounts and the figures you enter by hand (prior-year carryovers, fund distributions); ticker.map holds dated renames and symbol lookups. Manual history goes in plain-text .tt files for anything a broker didn't export — an old position, a transfer in, a custody move. tjs migrate converts a project from an older version.

Rules with receipts

Every rule cites its source

Tax software usually asks you to trust it. taxjson ships REFERENCES.md: each computation names the section of the Act and the CRA or IRS document it follows — and each thing it deliberately does not do names the provision it skips.

Superficial loss, across accounts

s.54 / s.40(2)(g) / s.53(1)(f), sized with CRA's formula for each sale: the least of the units sold, the units bought in the 61-day window and the units held at day 30. The RRSP and TFSA are affiliated persons (s.251.1); a loss denied because the plan repurchased is reported as permanently denied, not quietly deferred.

Identical properties

s.47 average cost, IT-387R2: a US listing and its TSX listing are the same pool; a CDR is not. Trades count on the settlement date, as T4037 says.

Renames are events

A ticker change happens on a date: the pool, its cost and the acquisition dates move to the new symbol then. A later trade in the old ticker is never silently pooled — tjs renames lists it and ticker.map says whether it is the same shares or another company.

Options and warrants

s.49(1)–(4), IT-479R: a written option's premium is a gain in the year written, a buy-back a loss in its own year, an assignment folds the premium into the shares, and an exercised warrant's cost goes into the shares (s.49(3)). tjs option-boundary says whether a filed year needs a T1-ADJ.

Income in the right year

A dividend belongs to the year it is paid (s.82(1)); a Canadian trust's distribution to the year it became payable (s.104(13)), dated by its record date. Payments in lieu, return of capital and reinvested distributions each have their own rule.

Corporate actions

Splits, spin-offs (s.86.1), mergers with the s.85.1 rollover election, return of capital — with the election recorded and the filing reminder printed at every run.

Minimum tax and carry-forwards

tjs amt shows the 2024+ AMT line by line with its 7-year carryover (s.120.2); close-year records the year's carry-forwards and next year's estimate, carryover and handoff start from them.

US (experimental)

IRC §1091 wash sales across your accounts, Rev. Rul. 2008-5 for IRA repurchases, lots carried between your own accounts, §355 spin-offs and §356 boot per lot (Reg. 1.358-2, 1.356-1(b)), Form 8949 codes.

Crypto

Property, per the CRA guide: every swap, spend and gift is a disposition at fair value; a network fee paid in the coin is one too; identical-property pooling and the superficial-loss rule apply per coin.

tjs tax-logic prints every rule taxjson applies for your project's country, one line each, with your settings filled in. It is the spec: each rule has an id the tests cite, the build fails on a rule with no test, and Canadian and US rules never mix — a project has one country, and the other country's settings and commands are refused.

Verification

Checked against the one thing that can't lie: your broker's positions

Internal reports can all agree and still be wrong. taxjson sanity compares the positions the books say you hold against the positions your broker says you hold, account by account. Any difference is either a trade that hasn't reached the CSV yet or a booking problem — there is no third kind. taxjson run finishes with that check once each account names its broker positions file (holdings = [...]).

6,500+ tests

Every fix lands with a regression test that failed before it, tagged with the tax-logic rule it pins. No mocks for tax math.

Two full audits

After eight review rounds and a pre-release security audit, two full-coverage audits of the whole codebase: about 1,300 findings, then 1,600 in the re-audit that also checked the first round's fixes. Every confirmed finding reproduced, fixed and pinned.

Fuzzers and mutation testing

Three property fuzzers (gains conserved under the superficial-loss rule, transfer netting, split-during-settlement books) run thousands of random ledgers per run; mutation testing of both engines finds the boundaries no test pins.

Every figure traced

tjs audit recomputes each disposition from its broker row through FX, the cost pool and the loss rule, and ties it to the reports; check-filed recomputes every year you locked and reports drift.

Dates checked

tjs check-dates holds every trade and settlement date to its market's calendar; edge-cases lists what lands on a year end or a 30-day boundary, and why.

Published limits

KNOWN_ISSUES.md lists what is out of scope and what to do by hand, with the citation.

Commands

One pipeline, a few dozen views on it

Set up

init scaffolds a project, migrate converts an older one, fetch downloads Questrade and IBKR activity (plugin), elect records corporate-action elections.

Build the books

run parses, converts, pools, applies corporate actions and the wash pass, and ends with the broker cross-check; crypto-sends and find-missing-history settle what the exports leave open.

Summaries

sum for the year by form line, estimate and amt for the tax and minimum tax, instalments, stats for wins and losses by asset class, fx-cash.

Positions

list per account (or as of a date), shares combined across accounts.

Row listings

events, trades, divs, dil, roc, gains, fees, leaps and transfers: the rows behind the numbers, over any window.

Totals by type

winners ranked, and per-ticker totals: divs-sum, dil-sum, roc-sum, trades-sum, fees-sum, ccd-sum, leaps-sum.

Before you trade

wash-radar, sell-check and buy-check answer "is this safe from the superficial-loss rule today?"; harvest lists the losses worth taking; scan flags tax-inefficient holdings; watch reports changes from cron.

Before you file

checklist runs the filing checklist with every step auto-detected; behind it form-export for Schedule 3 / Form 8949, reconcile-slips against the T5008, t1135, carryover, option-boundary, close-year to lock what you filed, check-filed, and handoff to start the next year from it.

Explain and check

audit justifies every gain, tax-logic states every rule, wash-sales lists each denied loss, renames, splits and spinoffs show each event, sanity compares with your broker, edge-cases and check-dates look at the boundaries.

Tools

redact strips personal details from an export so you can share it; help shows the grouped list (--all: both countries' commands).

tjs with no command lists every command under these headings, one line each — inside a project only your country's (tjs help --all shows both). Every command takes -h; most take --json. The full table is in the README; a twelve-slide overview is in the deck.

Install

Your first books in ten minutes

bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://taxjson.com/install.sh)"

Installs the stable release into ~/.local/share/taxjson with its own Python environment and links taxjson and its short name tjs into ~/.local/bin. Re-run to upgrade. Needs git and Python 3.9+; nothing else. Add _ --channel beta (or latest, dev for the development branch, or a version like v0.16.0) to pick another channel; the installer remembers it, so re-running upgrades along the same channel.

With the Questrade / IBKR auto-fetch plugin (an install that has it keeps it on upgrade):

bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://taxjson.com/install.sh)" _ --with-fetch
mkdir -p ~/taxes/2026 && cd ~/taxes/2026
tjs init --country canada   # or usa; taxjson.toml, ticker.map, inputs/
# drop broker CSVs into inputs/margin/, inputs/rrsp/, …
tjs run                     # books, reports, wash pass, broker check
tjs sum                     # the year, by Schedule 3 line
tjs wash-radar              # "can I sell this at a loss today?"
tjs checklist               # at year end: is everything ready to file?

Prefer a clone? git clone https://github.com/taxjson/taxjson && cd taxjson && scripts/dev-setup.sh. Already have a project from an older version? tjs migrate moves its files into the current layout.

Privacy and security

Nothing personal leaves the machine

Local by construction

Parsing, pooling and the wash pass run on your computer — no account, no server, no web UI; just commands and files. The pipeline's only network calls are FX rates from the Bank of Canada (Yahoo Finance before May 2007, for currencies the Bank does not publish, and for a US-dollar base) and crypto prices for rows the export didn't price. TAXJSON_OFFLINE=1 forbids both.

No credentials in the core

The core holds no broker client and reads no broker credential. Auto-fetch is the separate, optional taxjson-fetch plugin: it talks only to your broker, keeps its token outside your project, and sends it nowhere else. harvest and scan --online look up current prices, so Yahoo sees those symbols.

The project guards itself

A personal-data scan runs in every build and as the pre-push hook, fails closed, and reads a private denylist kept outside the repository. tjs redact strips the account numbers, names and contact details it recognises from an export while keeping every row shape — review the copy before you share it.

SECURITY.md lists every network call and how to report a vulnerability. The core has no third-party runtime dependencies.

Frequently asked

Before you file

taxjson is a calculator, not tax advice. It applies published rules to the rows you give it and shows its work; the return is yours, and a professional should review anything unusual — elections, cross-border reorganisations, business-income questions.
Does it send my data anywhere?

No. Parsing, pooling and the wash pass all run locally. The pipeline's only network calls are FX rates from the Bank of Canada (Yahoo Finance before May 2007, for currencies the Bank does not publish, and for a US-dollar base) and crypto prices from Yahoo Finance for rows the export didn't price, and TAXJSON_OFFLINE=1 disables both. harvest and scan --online look up current prices, so Yahoo sees those ticker symbols; the optional fetch plugin talks only to your broker. SECURITY.md lists every egress.

Canada or US?

Canada is the supported product: average-cost ACB with the superficial-loss rule and settlement-date timing, validated against real multi-account books and the brokers' own positions. A US engine ships too — FIFO with §1091 wash sales across your accounts, lots carried between your own accounts, short/long-term classification and Form 8949 codes — but it is experimental: unit-tested, never validated on a real account. Use it as a draft, and if you have real US exports, tjs redact them and send them in. A project is one country; the two rule sets never mix.

How do I know I'm ready to file?

tjs checklist. It is the filing checklist as a command: each step is proved by running the command behind it — a clean run, the broker cross-check, no missing cost basis, every disposition audited, the T5008 reconciled, no year-end option needing an amendment — and the steps only you can confirm are marked with --done. --walk takes you through the open ones.

What about the tax years I already filed?

tjs close-year locks the numbers you filed; check-filed and every later run flag any drift so an engine fix or a late row shows up as a candidate for an adjustment rather than a silent change. The lock also records what next year needs — the positions and cost at Dec 31, the trades that settle in January, the net capital loss and minimum-tax carry-forwards — and next year's tjs handoff checks the new project starts from exactly that.

My broker isn't listed.

Parsers are small, self-contained modules with a row-accounting contract; a new one is an afternoon's work given a real export. Run tjs redact on yours — it strips account numbers and names while keeping every row shape — and attach the copy to an issue. The plain-text .tt format covers you in the meantime.

How is it verified?

A 6,500+-test suite that every change must pass, each tax test tagged with the rule it pins; three property fuzzers and mutation testing; eight review rounds and two full-coverage audits (about 1,300 and 1,600 findings) logged in the changelog; and — the one that matters — tjs sanity against your broker's own position export. Every computation names its ITA / CRA / IRC source in REFERENCES.md, and tjs tax-logic states each rule as the code applies it.

I used an older version of taxjson.

Run tjs migrate in the project: the old per-purpose files (yf_ticker.map, crypto_ticker.map, claimed_losses.txt, distributions.map and the like) become lines in ticker.map and tables in taxjson.toml, and each old file is renamed .migrated. Every other command stops, naming the file, until you do. --dry-run shows what would change.
